U.S. vs. China Military Expenditure

Author

Antik Salimullah

Documentation

I pulled the data from https://www.sipri.org/databases/milex?utm, you are allowed to download the data set straight from the website. The original SIPRI Military Expenditure dataset (Constant (2024) US$ worksheet) contains, 193 rows (including regional headings and a blank row), 80 columns (including 77 year columns from 1949–2025).

In terms of transforming from wide to long, I ran into issues with my code after filtering for China and the US because when using “-country” it takes everything that’s not a country and put’s them in to the rows therefore “Notes” and “…2” fell into those rows. I had to specify “cols = ‘1949’:‘2025’” in order to only pull numerical years into rows. From there I got rid of the irrelevant columns. I then chose to round the numbers to two decimals and then check for missing values, which China had 40. I then removed those 40 NA’s from the dataset.

Finally, I created a line graph and a average using ggplot2 and the mean function in R to compare military spending trends over time and better understand China’s growing military expenditure relative to the United States.

Military Expenditure Trends Over Time

ggplot(sipri_long, aes(
  x = as.numeric(Year),
  y = Military_Expenditure,
  color = Country
)) +
  geom_line() +
  scale_y_continuous(labels = scales::comma) +
  labs(
    title = "U.S. vs. China Military Expenditure",
    x = "Year",
    y = "Military Expenditure (In Millions USD)",
    color = "Country"
  )

Interpretation of Military Expenditure Trends

As you can see the US military has significantly spent more than China. Although China only has half the amount of data in years that the US has, the trend for the US still exceeds that of China. In 2025 the US exceeded Chinese military spending by 600 billion (US 900 billion, China 300 billion). Another point is that the US expenditure fluctuates up and down quite a lot through out the years but trends upwards.

Conclusion

The analysis shows that the United States has consistently spent more on its military than China. The US on average has spent 695 billion compared to China’s 126 million. However, China’s military spending has steadily increased over time, reflecting its growing influence as a global power.
